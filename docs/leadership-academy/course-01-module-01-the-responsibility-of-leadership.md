# What It Means to Lead at BTW

## Module 1: The Responsibility of Leadership

**Program tier:** Emerging Leader  
**Estimated time:** 20 minutes  
**Module type:** Written lesson with reflection  
**Required:** Yes  
**Status:** Draft

## Purpose

Leadership is not simply a title, a promotion, or the authority to tell other people what to do. At BTW Global, leadership is a responsibility to care for people, establish clear standards, make sound decisions, and help the team succeed.

This module introduces the responsibilities that come with leading others and establishes the foundation for the rest of the course.

## Learning Objectives

After completing this module, the participant should be able to:

1. Explain what leadership means at BTW Global.
2. Describe the difference between authority and responsibility.
3. Identify the people and business responsibilities of a leader.
4. Recognize behaviors that build or damage trust.
5. Explain why compassion and accountability must coexist.
6. Identify one leadership behavior they want to strengthen.

## Opening Question

Before continuing, consider this question:

> When employees describe their experience working for you, what do you want them to say?

There is no single correct response. The purpose of this question is to begin thinking about the impact a leader has on other people.

## 1. Leadership Is a Responsibility

A job title may give someone authority, but authority alone does not make that person an effective leader.

Leadership begins when a person accepts responsibility for:

* The way employees are treated.
* The standards the team follows.
* The clarity of expectations.
* The quality and accuracy of the work.
* The development of employees.
* The decisions made within the team.
* The culture created through everyday behavior.
* The results the team produces.

Leaders influence how employees experience the workplace. A leader can create clarity, trust, belonging, and confidence. A leader can also create confusion, resentment, fear, and disengagement.

That influence exists whether the leader is thinking about it or not.

At BTW Global, leadership means using that influence intentionally and responsibly.

## 2. Leadership Is About People and Results

Effective leadership is not a choice between caring about people and achieving results. Leaders are responsible for both.

A leader who focuses only on results may achieve short-term goals while damaging trust, morale, retention, and teamwork.

A leader who focuses only on keeping everyone comfortable may avoid necessary feedback, allow standards to decline, and leave strong employees carrying an unfair share of the work.

BTW leaders are expected to balance compassion with accountability.

Compassion means:

* Listening before making assumptions.
* Treating employees with dignity.
* Recognizing that people may be facing challenges outside of work.
* Providing guidance, support, and reasonable opportunities to improve.
* Correcting mistakes without belittling the person.

Accountability means:

* Establishing clear expectations.
* Applying standards consistently.
* Addressing problems promptly.
* Following through on commitments.
* Documenting concerns when appropriate.
* Expecting employees and leaders to take ownership of their actions.

Compassion without accountability can become avoidance.

Accountability without compassion can become control.

Strong leadership requires both.

## 3. Leaders Establish the Standard

Employees pay attention to what leaders consistently do, tolerate, recognize, and correct.

If a leader expects punctuality but regularly arrives late, the leader weakens the standard.

If a leader speaks about teamwork but permits disrespect, employees learn that teamwork is optional.

If a leader tells employees to take responsibility but blames others when something goes wrong, employees notice the contradiction.

Leaders establish standards through:

* Their own conduct.
* The expectations they communicate.
* The behavior they recognize.
* The problems they address.
* The decisions they make when a situation is difficult.

Leading by example does not mean a leader must be perfect. It means the leader is willing to meet the same standards they expect from others, acknowledge mistakes, and correct their own behavior.

## 4. Fairness Builds Trust

Employees do not expect every situation to have the same outcome. They do expect decisions to be made honestly, consistently, and without favoritism.

Fairness requires leaders to:

* Apply the same core standards to everyone.
* Base decisions on relevant facts.
* Listen to the people involved.
* Avoid making decisions based on friendships, personal preferences, or assumptions.
* Explain decisions when it is appropriate to do so.
* Protect confidential information.
* Ask for help when they are unsure.

Consistency does not mean ignoring individual circumstances. It means using a reliable decision-making process and being able to explain why a decision was made.

Favoritism damages trust even when the leader does not intend to show it. When employees believe that relationships matter more than performance or conduct, morale and accountability suffer.

## 5. Leaders Teach and Develop Others

One of a leader's most important responsibilities is helping employees become more capable and confident.

When an employee makes a mistake, the leader should first determine what happened and why.

Consider:

* Was the expectation clear?
* Was the employee properly trained?
* Did the employee have the necessary tools and information?
* Was this a mistake, a skill gap, or a deliberate choice?
* Has the concern happened before?
* What response will help correct the problem and protect the standard?

Coaching does not mean ignoring poor performance. It means addressing the issue in a way that gives the employee clarity and a reasonable opportunity to improve.

Effective coaching focuses on the behavior, its impact, the expected standard, and the next step.

## 6. Leaders Do Not Have to Know Everything

Leadership does not require having every answer.

Responsible leaders:

* Listen to employees who may understand the work differently.
* Ask questions before deciding.
* Seek guidance when a situation exceeds their knowledge or authority.
* Involve Human Resources when appropriate.
* Change direction when better information becomes available.
* Admit mistakes and correct them.

Asking for help is not a leadership failure. Pretending to know something and making an avoidable poor decision can be.

Humility allows leaders to keep learning and gives employees permission to contribute ideas.

## 7. Everyday Leadership

Leadership is most visible in everyday moments.

It is visible when:

* An employee makes a mistake.
* The department is busy and under pressure.
* Two employees are in conflict.
* A high performer breaks a rule.
* An employee asks for help.
* A decision is unpopular.
* The leader realizes they were wrong.
* No senior leader is watching.

These moments reveal whether leadership principles are genuine or merely words.

## Scenario: The High Performer

Jordan is one of the department's most productive employees. Jordan regularly exceeds production expectations and is someone the supervisor relies on during busy periods.

Jordan has also started arriving late and speaking disrespectfully to newer employees. The supervisor has not addressed it because Jordan is difficult to replace and consistently produces strong results.

Consider:

1. What message does the supervisor's silence send to Jordan?
2. What message does it send to the rest of the team?
3. How could the supervisor address the behavior while recognizing Jordan's contributions?
4. Which BTW leadership responsibilities apply to this situation?

### Recommended Leadership Response

The supervisor should address the behavior promptly and privately. The conversation should recognize Jordan's contributions while making it clear that strong performance does not excuse conduct that violates team standards.

The supervisor should:

1. Describe the observed behavior using specific facts.
2. Explain how the behavior affects the team and workplace.
3. Restate the expectations for attendance and respectful conduct.
4. Listen for relevant information or circumstances.
5. Establish the required improvement and follow-up.
6. Document the conversation when appropriate.

This response protects fairness, accountability, respect, and the team's trust.

## Key Takeaways

* Leadership is a responsibility, not simply a title.
* Leaders are responsible for both people and results.
* Compassion and accountability should coexist.
* Employees learn standards by watching what leaders do and tolerate.
* Fairness and consistency build trust.
* Leaders coach, teach, and correct without belittling.
* Responsible leaders listen, ask for help, and acknowledge mistakes.
* Leadership is demonstrated through everyday decisions and behavior.

## Knowledge Check

### Question 1

Which statement best reflects leadership at BTW Global?

A. A leader's primary responsibility is keeping employees happy.  
B. A leader must choose between supporting people and achieving results.  
C. A leader is responsible for caring for people while maintaining clear standards.  
D. A leader should avoid correcting high performers because their output is valuable.

**Correct answer:** C

### Question 2

True or false:

Consistency means every employee situation must have exactly the same outcome.

**Correct answer:** False

**Explanation:** Consistency means using fair standards and a reliable decision-making process while considering relevant facts and individual circumstances.

### Question 3

Which action is the best example of coaching?

A. Ignoring a first mistake so the employee does not feel uncomfortable.  
B. Correcting the employee publicly so the team learns from the example.  
C. Explaining the observed behavior, its impact, the expected standard, and the next step.  
D. Taking over the employee's work without discussing the problem.

**Correct answer:** C

### Question 4

True or false:

Asking for help can demonstrate responsible leadership.

**Correct answer:** True

### Question 5

A high-performing employee repeatedly violates a workplace standard. What should the leader do?

A. Ignore the issue because the employee produces strong results.  
B. Apply the standard fairly while recognizing the employee's contributions.  
C. Wait until another employee complains.  
D. Immediately remove the employee without gathering facts.

**Correct answer:** B
